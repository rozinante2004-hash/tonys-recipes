# The instructional clip — "How to add a recipe" (started 16 Nov 2026)

Tony, 16 Nov 2026: "I would like to create a new 60-seconds instructional clip. Same infographic style as the last one,
but this time, it should also be humoristic and funny. The objective is to teach a new user how to add a recipe to
their recipe collection. I was thinking something along the lines of "Let's say you are a witch, and you want to
concoct a new potion (show a cartoon/comics/anime witch in a pointy black hat, in relevant surroundings (basement,
lab, bobbling potions. There should also be a distinct looking black cat)). But you do not have a good recipe, so you
go scanning through witchcraft books (and here goes the option to take a photo), or go online to your favorite witchy
website (Import from URL, copy paste or click the extension) or confide in a witch friend (add manually). Now, let's
assume that you are not really a witch, but simply someone who is looking for a good recipe for a french onion soup.
You just change your hat and do exactly the same (change to a normal hat, mundane kitchen). The same cat makes a
second appearance, indication that you may still be a witch, and winks meaningfully to the audience. Use the same
closing line as in the previous clip … Please ask me questions and create 3 script options for me to review."

How the first clip was made, and the rules it set, are in `docs/promo-clip.md` — the same apply here: our own
pictures and sound, the app's colours and fonts, real app screens (`tests/screen-walk.js`), ElevenLabs voices (Jon in
English, Nimrod in Hebrew), square 1080 × 1080, a caption for every sentence, never a web page's photo — and (Tony,
16 Nov) **the very first frame is the logo**, the frame WhatsApp shows before the clip plays.

## What every option has in common
- **Frame 1: the logo**, large and sharp, in the middle — then it shrinks and lands as the label on a potion bottle on
  the witch's shelf, and the lab fades in around it.
- **The ways in, by their names in the app** (➕ Add Recipe): 📷 Scan recipe (camera/photo) — the spellbook; 🔗 Import
  from URL, ✍️ Paste free-hand text and the 🧩 browser extension's Save-recipe button — the witchy website; ✏️ Fill in
  manually — the witch friend. Each shown on the real app screen, the button tapped in close-up, the recipe appearing.
- **The made-up potion** on the app's screens: "Moonlight Elixir" — 3 drops of moonlight, a pinch of dragon pepper,
  1 cup of rainwater (collected on a Tuesday), stir anticlockwise until it hums. The made-up witchy website:
  "Cauldron Corner" (no real site).
- **The witch:** drawn by us, flat cartoon in the app's style — a tall, patched, crooked pointy black hat, her face
  mostly under its brim (so she can be "you"). **The cat:** round and black, one gold eye and one green, a white tip on
  a tail curled like a question mark, a little silver star on its collar — the same cat in both halves, so it is
  recognised at once. (Drawn so it is no famous cartoon cat.)
- **The hat swap:** a puff of purple smoke — the pointy hat becomes an ordinary one, the basement a bright everyday
  kitchen, the cauldron a soup pot, the potion bottle a bowl of French onion soup.
- **The end:** the logo, "All your recipes. One place. For good.", mykitchennotes.community, "By invitation only. For
  now" — as in the first clip; spoken: "My Kitchen Notes. All your recipes, in one place, for good."
- **Length:** about 130–140 spoken words — about 60 seconds at Jon's pace (the first clip: 61 s of speech, 70 s in all).
- **What can be drawn:** the characters move as the first clip's pictures did — sliding, bouncing, blinking, the
  steam and bubbles, the smoke puff, the hat flying off, the wink — not frame-by-frame animation.
